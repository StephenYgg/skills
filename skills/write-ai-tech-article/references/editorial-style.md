# Editorial Style

## Audience

Write for ordinary Chinese readers who are interested in technology but may not know the underlying jargon. Preserve professional depth while making the mechanism understandable.

## Editorial Position

- Lead with a question, change, conflict, or consequence that genuinely exists in the evidence.
- State a clear judgment. Do not hide behind a list of facts or a neutral-sounding recap.
- Support the judgment with data, documented behavior, expert statements, experiments, or comparable cases.
- Explain why the development happened, what changed, who is affected, and what remains uncertain.
- Translate technical concepts into consequences without replacing them with vague analogies.
- Prefer one well-developed angle over several shallow angles.

Use the depth and reporting discipline found in strong 36Kr and WIRED features and the technical skepticism found in high-quality Hacker News discussions. Do not copy their wording, structure, house style, or any named author's voice.

## Voice

- Sound informed, calm, direct, and curious.
- Use short-to-medium paragraphs with varied sentence length.
- Use concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Separate observation from interpretation with phrases such as `公开资料显示`, `这意味着`, `更值得注意的是`, or `目前仍无法确认` only when they accurately describe the evidence.
- Use first person only for a real opinion or experience supplied by the user. Never fabricate firsthand use, interviews, or access.
- Explain a necessary English term on first use; avoid stacking acronyms.

## Depth Pattern

For an article, prefer this flexible progression:

1. Open with the concrete event and why it matters now.
2. Explain the technology or business mechanism.
3. Introduce evidence, numbers, comparisons, or a case.
4. Identify the tradeoff, limitation, or counterargument.
5. End with a bounded judgment and its implication for readers.

Do not force this structure when another order better serves the thesis.

For a short post:

1. State the development in one sentence.
2. Give the most important evidence or mechanism.
3. End with one useful judgment, implication, or unresolved question.

## Anti-Slop Rules

Remove or rewrite:

- generic openings such as `在这个日新月异的时代`;
- empty conclusions such as `未来可期`, `值得关注`, or `让我们拭目以待`;
- repeated statements that a technology is important without showing why;
- ungrounded superlatives such as `颠覆`, `革命性`, `史上最强`, or `彻底改变`;
- fake quotations, invented user reactions, fabricated scenes, or made-up statistics;
- paragraphs that only translate a product announcement without analysis;
- excessive rhetorical questions, exclamation marks, bullet lists, or slogan-like fragments;
- technical terminology used as decoration rather than explanation.

## Editing Pass

After drafting, test every paragraph:

- What new fact, mechanism, implication, counterpoint, or judgment does this paragraph add?
- Is the claim supported by the evidence ledger?
- Could a general reader understand the consequence without losing the technical meaning?
- Can any sentence be made more specific or shorter?

Delete paragraphs that add no new information.
